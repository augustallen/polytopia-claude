# Strategy: Domination rush (1 opponent, small map)
- Objective: eliminate the opponent by capturing all their cities. Choose the next operation by what you can take AND hold while preserving your recruitment base. Before committing to a siege, name the garrison-breaking force, its arrival turn, the occupier, and the defenders of your threatened cities. If the force is not ready, use the army to intercept approaching enemies, protect expansion, or take a weaker city. Reassess after every enemy reply: if you are losing units or cities without bringing a capture closer, withdraw exposed units and change the operation. Prefer the capital only when its capture is comparably achievable. Stop expansion and growth only when the existing army can finish the remaining cities.
- Opening: your tribe's starting tech is listed in the header (Imperius: Organization). On the first turn, harvest two fruit (4 stars) to reach capital level 2 and move the starting Warrior toward a nearby village or a useful scouting frontier. Train a second Warrior when it accelerates a separate village capture or answers a threat, not merely because a support slot is free. Early on, claim nearby villages that give income, forward recruitment tiles, or deny the opponent. Split early scouting across useful unexplored approaches rather than sending every unit along the same route; enemy contacts are evidence about direction, not proof of the capital's location. When an enemy city is discovered close by, check whether current units and near-term reinforcements can kill its garrison, enter, and survive before committing to an assault; a short distance alone does not justify an all-in. If walls or durable defenders already make the assault unproductive, secure accessible villages and their approaches while completing the quickest supported Giant or siege plan. Do not wait beside the enemy city for that investment to mature.
- Technology: buy Riding when you can fund a useful Rider soon without delaying a village capture or valuable city reward (Warriors explore fresh fog fine; Riders shine at crossing revealed ground, reinforcing, and attack-then-escape). Roads when specific placements shorten reinforcement or attack routes. Hunting -> Archery provides supporting damage. When current attacks cannot break a garrison efficiently, compare the earliest supported operation using existing units, a Giant level-up, Forestry -> Mathematics and a Catapult, or the remaining Smithery prerequisites and Swordsmen; include research, training, travel, protection, and occupation in the comparison, and buy the force needed for the current field battle while assembling the siege force. A Catapult has 0 defence: before training one or ending its turn, inspect enemy move-and-attack reach, ranged attacks, roads, and approaches opened by killing your screen, and choose a position where it survives until its first useful shot (an adjacent friend does not cover every approach; melee screens do not stop ranged fire). If no protected deployment exists, train a durable defender or supporting attacker first, or deploy the Catapult farther back; range to the target is useful only when the Catapult survives to fire. Never buy Philosophy, Meditation, Trade or Aquatism.
- Army: build the unit that supplies the missing role: Warriors for cheap capture and occupation, Riders for mobility and repeated attacks, Archers for supported ranged damage, Catapults/Giants or stronger units when basic attacks cannot break the defence. Buy Archers when their arrival enables useful focus fire against field units, protects cities, or completes a siege kill. Buy Shields and Defenders when durable garrisons free the mobile army or protect threatened recruitment cities; Defenders hold positions, so pair them with units that can kill attackers. Do not keep buying Riders for a stationary siege unless they have a specific interception, finishing, or occupation role. Stop recruiting only when additional units cannot improve either defence or the remaining conquest before victory.
- Economy: buy population toward a named level-up and reward, not just because a harvest is cheap. Free monuments (0 stars, +3 pop) are worth placing when the population brings Resources, support capacity or a Giant, on a tile you do not need for something better; one placement per monument. For a Giant plan, name the city, its remaining population, the affordable builds, and the resulting reward; recheck the city after Population Growth, since reaching level 4 does not automatically produce a Giant. Do not pursue score-only rewards or expensive temples. Buy tech before capturing in the same turn (tech gets pricier per city). Before ending the turn, fund the immediate operation: save a threatened city, kill its attackers, recruit missing combat roles, or complete a useful city reward. Check every city for useful recruitment, budgeting the purchases cumulatively. If a friendly occupant blocks needed recruitment, decide whether it can safely move, then refresh and train this turn. Research defensive or supporting units when those roles are missing. Any reserve must name the purchase, its full remaining cost, the recruitment city if applicable, and the intended turn; spend surplus beyond that reserve when it improves the position. Saving for an unspecified capture or "replacements" is not a purchase plan.
- Fighting: evaluate the whole exchange through the next enemy reply. Prioritise kills that save cities, remove dangerous attackers, or permit occupation, even when the opening hit takes more damage than it deals. Focus fire; earlier hits change later damage and retaliation. Use ranged softening before a melee finisher when it improves survival. Recover only when the expected healing and resulting position are more useful than attacking, screening, reinforcing, or retreating; do not repeatedly heal under attacks that erase the recovery, withdraw toward support or remove the attacker instead. Full health is not a prerequisite for a useful attack. Preserve units for a named next action, not merely to avoid losing hp.
- Sieging a city: before firing at a city, name the unit that will occupy it this turn, its approach tile, and the enemy units that could kill it before capture; reserve that unit's movement or attack. If entry is not possible, keep the occupier outside avoidable enemy attack reach until the breaking force arrives; staging must have a named purpose and arrival turn, and proximity to the enemy city alone is not progress. A legal melee kill advances the attacker onto the target tile; a ranged kill leaves it empty, and moving onto the emptied city needs a fresh action list (omit end turn and use a remaining call for the entry). A besieging unit gets no Fortify or wall bonus. Remove nearby counterattackers and protect the route before committing. After a Rider attack, Escape when it preserves the unit or frees an attack lane, but never abandon a city tile you intend to capture next turn. Expect the opponent to re-train a defender inside whenever it can: kill the garrison and step in with a healthy unit in the same turn.
- Defence: before offensive moves, inspect every owned city for an enemy occupant and for enemies that can enter it or kill its garrison during the next enemy turn. Evict an enemy occupant before it captures, prioritising your last city and essential recruitment cities over unrelated kills or siege damage. Newly captured forward cities need a healthy garrison and nearby units able to kill approaching attackers; do not send the capturer away until that defence is replaced. A garrison alone is insufficient if several enemies can overwhelm it. Vacate a threatened recruitment tile only when the replacement can be trained this turn and survive the reply. Keep safe city tiles available for recruitment. If a forward city cannot be held or rescued, withdraw valuable units toward a defensible city and stop investing in the doomed position.
- City rewards and peace requests are answered by the harness (Workshop/Explorer, Resources, Population Growth, Super Unit; peace always declined): do not include reward indices, but account for their stars, population, support capacity and Giant placement when ordering actions.
